## Why build CueAudit

The initial version has a limited, testable job: match exact cue intervals, identify missing or added cues, and compare selected numeric, link, English-negation, and user-protected phrase anchors inside each corresponding cue. JSON and Markdown output make findings usable in a review or CI pipeline. A library caller can retain the whole original subtitle text when the candidate needs review.

A useful demonstration swaps two numbers between different cues. A file-wide number count still matches, while cue-local checks identify both affected intervals. Demo subtitles are synthetic test material, not evidence of model accuracy or production use.

The adoption hypothesis is that an independent Node.js 22+ CLI and library, with no provider calls, is easier to add after an existing cleanup step than another transcription or editing application. This is a hypothesis about usefulness, not a forecast of stars. [Existing alternatives](alternatives.md) already cover substantial parts of subtitle validation, transcription review, and translation quality; CueAudit's scope is the small preservation check between a source file and a revision.

## Limits and future observations

Passing means the configured checks found no issue. It does not prove semantic equivalence, correct transcription, audio alignment, or translation quality. Exact interval matching intentionally flags retiming and cue splitting/merging for review. English negation checks do not cover other languages, and unchanged anchor counts can coexist with changed meaning within a cue.

Future research should retain the retrieval time, archive commit, exact repository activity dates, and directly observed Trending entries. Compare like-for-like snapshots before discussing growth. Prioritize real issue reports and integration feedback over inventing benchmark claims or engagement.
